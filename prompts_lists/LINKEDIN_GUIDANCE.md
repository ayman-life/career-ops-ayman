

# How to cold email

> Source person: https://www.linkedin.com/in/joshua-jameson-rickard/recent-activity/all


Most international graduates in the UK are losing recruiter responses, hiring manager replies, and warm introductions on a single thing.

The message they send.

In the last 6 weeks I have received over 150 cold messages from international graduates and professionals. Many of them are excellent and I have responded with detailed free CV reviews. Some are not. The pattern is consistent and worth naming because the same pattern is costing people responses from actual recruiters and hiring managers, not just from me.

Three things that change whether senior professionals respond to your cold LinkedIn message.

1. Open with a greeting. Not "Lend me a job in London." Not "Can you get me a job." Start with "Hi [name]" or "Good morning [name]". This applies to me, to recruiters, to hiring managers, to anyone you cold-message. The absence of basic greeting is the single fastest reason senior professionals delete a message without reading further.

2. Give context in two sentences. Your nationality and visa status. Your degree and graduation year. Your target role and sector. Your specific ask. Senior professionals do not have time to interview you in DMs to figure out how to help. The candidates who get help are the ones who make help easy to give.

3. Wait. If a senior professional has not responded in 4 hours, it does not mean they are ignoring you. It means they are with a paying client, in a meeting, or with their family. Following up with "?" within hours signals that you do not understand professional rhythms. Wait 5 to 7 days minimum before any follow-up, and when you follow up, add value or new context rather than asking again.

The harder truth underneath this: the way you message a senior professional cold on LinkedIn is exactly the way you will eventually message recruiters at your target firms, hiring managers at your target companies, and partners at agencies who could refer you. The habits you build now compound across every professional interaction for the next 30 years of your career.

International graduates losing UK applications often blame the market, the CV, the visa. Sometimes the answer is simpler. The way you ask for help signals how you will perform once helped. Senior professionals read that signal in 7 seconds.

If you write to me clearly, with context, and with patience, I will continue offering free CV reviews where I can. That offer stands. But it is also the answer to a much bigger question about how to navigate UK professional culture.

Write like you mean it. People notice.
